### What the two losers were, measured

Both were pullback entries at a level, and in both the direction eventually came right. That is not what
made them lose.

| | #5 BUY | #9 SELL |
|---|---|---|
| my stop, and where it sat | 5049, under the last higher low 5052 | 5041, above the swing high 5040 |
| how far price went BEYOND it | **28.9 USD** (to 5020) | **58.0 USD** (to 5099) |
| did it then reach my target? | yes, at 17:40 | yes, at 22:05 |
| a stop that would have survived | 38.4 USD of risk, paying **+0.51 R** | 66.5 USD of risk, paying **+0.30 R** |

**The obvious conclusion is wrong.** I was about to write "my stops are too tight, put them past the
sweep". The numbers say that a stop wide enough to survive costs four to seven times the risk and pays
+0.3 to +0.5 R - against a tight stop that loses exactly 1.00 R when wrong and paid **+3.67 R** when
right. On these three fills the tight-stop, far-target geometry is worth far more than surviving the
sweep.

What the losers actually say is about **timing, not stop width**: in both I entered a retest while the
move against me had not finished. The stop was not in the wrong place; the entry was too early in the
sweep. That is a different thing to fix, and it needs more than three trades to see.
